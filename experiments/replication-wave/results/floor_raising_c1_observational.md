# C1 observational floor-raising test

Generated by `analysis/floor_raising_c1_observational.py`. C1 (baseline blinded) observational analog of the label-swap floor-raising test in `floor_raising_test.md`. For each judge J and each of the 10 prompts P, we form the J-authored response; record J's own composite (`self_score`); record the mean of the other three judges' composites for the same response (`baseline_mean`); and form `delta = self_score − baseline_mean`. The same negative-correlation pattern (judges over-reward their own outputs most strongly on outputs other judges rate weakest) would predict ρ(Δ, baseline) < 0.

## Interpretation

**Different baseline from the headline C1 self-preference.** The per-judge mean Δ in this table is not the same as the abstract's within-judge self-preference gap. Here Δ compares a judge's rating of its own authored response to the other three judges' consensus for that same response. By this between-judge baseline, all four judges rate their own outputs slightly above peer consensus, including Kimi (+0.56), even though Kimi's within-judge C1 self-gap is strongly negative.

The floor-raising pattern is only suggestive in C1 because each judge has n=10 own-authored prompts. Claude and GPT have negative point estimates similar in sign to the causal label-swap mechanism, Gemini is near null, and Kimi is negative but very uncertain. The native label-swap experiment remains the cleaner causal test of displayed-label effects.
